WEEK 3 - PHASE 2: LOAD BALANCING
================================
Goal (professor's review "Project review phase 2 - RRH balancing and load balancing", thesis sections
3.2.4 and 3.3.4): starting from the Minimise Delay assignment of phase 1, balance the pools so the network
is more resilient to faults: (A) by traffic (GBph, virtualised scenario, no pool capacity limit) and
(B) by number of RRHs; compare with the delay-only assignment, for normal / few / many pools.

2. DECISIONS AND REASONS
------------------------
- Scenario = phase 1 reference: 200 real RRHs (seed 42), geographic traffic pattern (120 residential,
  30 office, 50 mixed), 25 candidate pools, RTT <= 200 us (d_max = 20 km). The active pools of every K
  come from the phase-1 backward elimination (K = 19 = reference, P06 P07 P10 P11 P24 P25 off).
- K cases: normal 19, few 8, many 25 (proposed in week 2; all three serve every RRH at 200 us).
- 24 h profiles: thesis fig. E.1, digitised (same values as Anas's branch). Index h = hour ending at h:00.
- Busy-hour traffic of an RRH (no per-site traffic is public): per merged station 45 Mbps if 4G
  (3 sectors x 20 MHz x 1.5 b/s/Hz, 50 % load) + 150 Mbps if 5G (3 x 100 MHz x 5 b/s/Hz, 10 % load),
  times the number of stations merged at the site (week 1). Converted to GBph (1 Mbps for 1 h = 0.45 GB),
  the unit of the thesis. Result: 20 to 527 GBph per RRH, mean 137 GBph.
  GOPS were not used: with no pool capacity limit the number of cards is proportional to the traffic.
- Balancing hour: 16:00 = maximum of the sum of the three profiles (review a). The network as a whole
  peaks at 22:00 because 60 % of the RRHs are residential.
- Algorithm A (traffic, review b-d): 1st iteration: RRHs with one possible pool (none at 200 us);
  2nd iteration, order: fewest possible pools, then highest traffic at 16:00, then nearest possible pool;
  each RRH goes to its possible pool with the lowest load; ties -> nearest. Load updated after each RRH.
- Algorithm B (number of RRHs, review e-f): same first iteration; order: fewest possible pools, then nearest
  possible pool; each RRH goes to the possible pool with the fewest RRHs; ties -> nearest.
- Hour dependence (review 2b): algorithm A repeated at each of the 24 hours and compared with the 16:00
  result, which is the one adopted.
- All residential / all office (review 1b, 2c): every RRH given the same profile, same peak traffic.
- Extra: a distance-aware variant of A (a pool is allowed only if it is at most s km farther than the
  RRH's nearest possible pool) to show the balance / distance trade-off. Not part of the review algorithm.


3. RESULTS (reference K = 19, balancing at 16:00)
-------------------------------------------------
                          only distance    A: traffic     B: number of RRHs
  load CV at 16:00        1.11             0.04           0.26
  pool load at 16:00      62 - 3941 GBph   875 - 986      390 - 1201
  RRHs per pool           2 - 33           7 - 15         10 - 11
  mean / max distance     2.0 / 6.8 km     10.3 / 20.0    7.5 / 19.4
  mean RTT                19.9 us          102.5 us       75.2 us
  RRHs moved              -                182 of 200     157 of 200
  24 h average cap. CV    0.90             0.19           0.27
  multiplexing gain       1.14             1.20           1.20
- Before balancing the two central pools P19 and P01 carry 3.9 and 3.3 TBph at 16:00, while P12 carries
  62 GBph (review 1a: CV 1.00 at 8:00, 1.07 at 15:00, 0.84 at 21:00).
- Network load (review 1c): minimum 3.6 TBph at 7:00, 17.0 TBph at 16:00, maximum 22.1 TBph at 22:00.
  Sum of the RRH peaks 27.4 TBph -> network multiplexing gain 1.24.
- All residential / all office: one profile multiplies every RRH by the same factor, so the balancing at
  any hour gives exactly the same assignment for both (CV 0.94 before, 0.00 after). The real mix is harder
  to balance (1.11 -> 0.04) because office and residential RRHs peak at different hours.
- Hour dependence: balancing at another hour moves 124 (11:00) to 174 (3:00) of the 200 RRHs. With the
  adopted 16:00 assignment the load CV stays between 0.04 and 0.37 over the day (delay only: 0.82 - 1.21).
- Price of balancing: every pool is reachable from almost every RRH (20 km reach, 30 km box), so the
  balancing sends RRHs across the city: 51 % of the links are longer than 10 km with A, 30 % with B
  (0 % with delay only). Still inside 200 us, but 5x the delay-only mean.
- Distance-aware variant: allowing at most 5 km over the nearest pool gives CV 0.58 with a 4.4 km mean;
  10 km gives CV 0.28 with 7.9 km; 15 km reaches the full balance (0.04).
- K comparison: few (8) pools are the easiest to balance (A: CV 0.00, 21 - 30 RRHs per pool) and keep the
  highest multiplexing gain (1.24); many (25) pools are the hardest (A: CV 0.10, B: 0.40) because some
  pools then have only 4 - 8 RRHs. Mean distance after balancing is 9 - 10 km in every case.
